## Preparation 
### Food
Since there are no supermarkets in the Icelandic highlands, it was important to plan our meals. After all, we didn’t want to go hungry on our vacation. For 10 days, that adds up to quite a lot. That means we had to pack a lot of meals, which can get really heavy if not planned properly. So the whole thing was a trade-off between price, weight, and nutrients.
Oh, and it’s important to note that Iceland has some import restrictions. For food, these are essentially the following:

- Plants and plant seeds
- Raw milk and raw eggs
- Raw meat and fish

The total weight of imported items must not exceed 10 kg, and their value must not exceed 25,000 ISK (approx. 170 €).
More detailed information is available, for example, at
[islandprotravel.de](https://islandprotravel.de).

Breakfast was always muesli with powdered milk and boiled water. Otherwise, we had light ready-to-eat meals or nuts. “Travellunch” is trekking food designed specifically for this purpose, but unfortunately it’s relatively expensive. We got our water daily from the campground or from flowing bodies of water.

The food alone, excluding water, weighed just under 8 kg for the 10 days.
To make things easier for customs officials—and for yourself—in the event of a customs check, you should carry a list of all food items and their weight and price. Fortunately, we didn’t need to do that.
